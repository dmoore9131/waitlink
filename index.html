How it works

One list. Open or don't.

What adding a number actually stops

Apps like Nomorobo and Robokiller stop a caller in three steps. First they check your own block list. Then they check a shared list of bad numbers, which this website does not have. Then the phone refuses the call before it rings. This site can only do the first step, plus a pattern check: lookalike names, a domain registered in the last 30 days, and premium 900 or 976 numbers.

Spam on a list is not a hacked account

Loan texts and recruiter mail mean your number or email is on a list. They do not mean someone is signed into the phone or Gmail. A fake PayPal note from a random Gmail, with a Word file attached, is a trick. Delete it. Do not open the file.

A real break-in looks different. Mail in Sent that you did not write. A password-reset email you did not request. A new device on the Google account. A forwarding rule you did not add.

Check it in this order. Open Google Security Checkup and look at devices, recent security activity, and the recovery phone and email. In Gmail, open Settings, See all settings, then Forwarding and Filters, and remove anything you did not create. On iPhone turn on Settings, Messages, Filter Unknown Senders, and Settings, Phone, Silence Unknown Callers. Report junk. Do not reply. This site still cannot read your inbox or hang up a call.

Saving an address here does not delete the email or hang up the call. It stops the next mistake. The next time you paste it, the page says don't open before you reply. You still block the number in the Phone app, and you still report the message in Gmail or Yahoo, because only those apps can refuse the next one. People on Reddit keep finding the same limit: scammers spoof a new number after you block one, so blocking a single number is not enough. Silence Unknown Callers on iPhone, and don't answer a number you don't know. Answering is what tells them the line is live.
